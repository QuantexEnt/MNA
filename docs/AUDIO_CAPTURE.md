# Audio Capture Specification

## Meeting Notes Assistant — V1

**Document Status:** Critical technical specification  
**Platform:** Windows 10/11  
**Primary meeting platform:** Microsoft Teams  
**Capture objective:** Capture the audio of the Teams meeting without independently recording the user's physical microphone.

---

# 1. Purpose

Audio capture is the most technically critical part of Meeting Notes Assistant.

The application must capture the audio associated with the Microsoft Teams meeting so that the resulting transcript represents what was actually part of the meeting.

The application is **not** intended to independently record the user's microphone.

The central requirement is:

> **Capture meeting audio, not private/local microphone speech.**

---

# 2. Core Requirement

When the user explicitly starts a note-taking session:

```text
Microsoft Teams
      ↓
Teams meeting audio
      ↓
Windows audio system
      ↓
Application audio capture
      ↓
Local recording
      ↓
Transcription
```

The application must not automatically begin capture merely because Teams is open or a meeting is detected.

---

# 3. Expected Audio Behavior

## 3.1 Other Participant Speaks

Scenario:

```text
Participant A speaks
        ↓
Teams receives/transmits audio
        ↓
Teams plays audio on user's computer
        ↓
Capture
        ↓
Transcript
```

Expected:

**Participant A's voice is captured.**

---

## 3.2 User Speaks While Unmuted

Scenario:

```text
User speaks
        ↓
Teams microphone is unmuted
        ↓
Voice is transmitted into Teams
        ↓
Teams meeting audio
        ↓
Capture
        ↓
Transcript
```

Expected:

**The user's spoken contribution should be captured.**

This is important because the application should represent the actual meeting conversation.

---

## 3.3 User Speaks While Muted

Scenario:

```text
User speaks
        ↓
Teams microphone is muted
        ↓
Voice is not transmitted into Teams
        ↓
No meeting audio contribution
        ↓
Should not be captured by the meeting-audio recorder
```

Expected:

**The user's muted/private speech should not appear in the meeting recording or transcript.**

This requirement is one reason the application should not simply record the physical microphone independently.

---

# 4. Capture Strategy

The preferred technical approach is to investigate and use Windows audio APIs, particularly:

**WASAPI**

and, where supported and appropriate:

**WASAPI Process Loopback Capture**

The purpose of Process Loopback is to isolate audio generated by a particular process rather than blindly recording the entire physical output device.

Conceptually:

```text
                 Windows Audio
                      │
       ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
       │              │              │
     Teams         YouTube       System sounds
       │
       ▼
Teams process audio
       │
       ▼
Process Loopback
       │
       ▼
Meeting Notes Assistant
```

The desired result is that the recorder primarily receives Teams audio.

---

# 5. Important Technical Caveat

The exact behavior of Microsoft Teams audio routing must be validated experimentally.

Do not assume that:

> Teams process audio = only meeting participant audio

There may be Teams-generated sounds such as:

- Ringing.
- Notification sounds.
- Join/leave sounds.
- UI sounds.
- Other audio associated with the Teams process.

The prototype must determine what audio is actually exposed by the Windows process loopback mechanism.

If process-specific capture cannot reliably isolate meeting speech, the implementation should document the limitation and evaluate the smallest alternative.

---

# 6. Do Not Capture the Physical Microphone by Default

The V1 architecture must not use:

```text
Physical Microphone
        ↓
Recorder
```

as the primary recording path.

This would violate the intended behavior because the microphone could contain speech that was not transmitted into the Teams meeting.

For example:

```text
Teams muted
+
User speaks locally
=
Do not independently record that speech
```

---

# 7. Headphones / Headsets

The capture implementation should support users who attend Teams meetings through:

- Laptop speakers.
- Wired headphones.
- USB headsets.
- Bluetooth headphones/headsets.

The recording must not depend on the sound physically coming out of the laptop speakers.

If Teams audio is routed to headphones, the capture mechanism should still receive the appropriate Teams process audio.

---

# 8. Echo Considerations

The application should not create an artificial microphone/speaker loop.

Do not route captured Teams audio back into Teams.

The architecture must be:

```text
Teams
  ↓
Audio output
  ↓
Capture
  ↓
Recorder
```

not:

```text
Teams
  ↓
Capture
  ↓
Virtual microphone
  ↓
Teams
```

The application is a passive observer of the local audio stream.

---

# 9. Audio Capture Lifecycle

## Start

Audio capture begins only after:

1. Teams meeting is detected.
2. User explicitly selects **Start Taking Notes**.

## During Meeting

The capture module:

- Continuously receives available Teams audio.
- Writes audio incrementally.
- Reports capture status.
- Does not block the UI.

## Stop

Capture stops when:

- User selects **End Meeting**, or
- The application reliably determines that the Teams meeting has ended.

## Finalization

After stopping:

1. Flush pending audio.
2. Finalize the recording file.
3. Close the audio stream.
4. Release Windows audio resources.
5. Make the final recording available to transcription.

---

# 10. Audio Format

The implementation should choose a format suitable for:

- Speech transcription.
- Reasonable file size.
- Reliable Windows capture.
- Simple processing.

A practical target is:

- PCM/WAV for the initial proof of concept.
- Sample rate appropriate for speech.
- Mono where stereo provides no meaningful benefit.
- Consistent sample format.

The exact format should be finalized after testing the selected Windows capture implementation and OpenAI transcription requirements.

Do not optimize for minimal file size before reliable capture is proven.

---

# 11. Buffering

The application should not hold an entire meeting in memory.

Use incremental buffering:

```text
Teams audio
    ↓
Audio buffer
    ↓
Small chunks
    ↓
Disk
```

This is important for long meetings.

The application should be able to handle at least:

- 30-minute meetings.
- 60-minute meetings.
- 2-hour meetings.

without requiring the entire recording to remain in RAM.

---

# 12. Audio File Management

During capture, the application may use:

- One continuously written recording file, or
- Multiple temporary chunks.

The simplest reliable implementation should be preferred.

If chunks are used:

```text
meeting/
├── audio/
│   ├── chunk_0001.wav
│   ├── chunk_0002.wav
│   └── chunk_0003.wav
```

After capture, chunks may be combined or processed individually.

The application must ensure that a partially written file is not mistaken for a completed recording.

---

# 13. Capture Status

The UI must clearly indicate capture state.

Possible states:

```text
NOT CAPTURING
CAPTURING
PAUSED
STOPPING
FINALIZING
CAPTURE ERROR
```

The UI should never display a recording indicator when the audio capture subsystem is not actually running.

---

# 14. User Control

The user should have clear controls:

```text
[ Start Taking Notes ]
```

and, during the session:

```text
[ Pause ]
[ End Meeting ]
```

Pause is optional if it adds significant implementation complexity.

The minimum requirement is:

```text
Start
Stop
```

---

# 15. Audio Capture Failure

If the application cannot capture Teams audio:

Display a clear message:

> **Unable to capture Teams meeting audio.**

Actions:

- Retry.
- Continue with manual notes.
- Cancel session.

The application must not claim that transcription is being recorded if capture failed.

---

# 16. Device Changes During Meeting

The application should attempt to tolerate Windows audio device changes.

Example:

```text
Meeting starts
     ↓
Headphones active
     ↓
Capture works
     ↓
User disconnects headphones
     ↓
Speakers become active
```

The application should attempt to continue where the Windows audio architecture allows.

If the capture stream becomes invalid, the application should:

1. Detect the failure.
2. Attempt recovery if safe.
3. Inform the user if recovery fails.
4. Preserve already captured audio.

Automatic device recovery is desirable but should not compromise core reliability.

---

# 17. Bluetooth Considerations

Bluetooth devices can introduce:

- Device changes.
- Profile changes.
- Sample-rate changes.
- Audio stream interruptions.

The application should not crash because a Bluetooth device disconnects.

The capture module should handle the audio-session error and attempt to recover or gracefully stop.

---

# 18. Teams Version Changes

The audio-capture implementation must not depend unnecessarily on undocumented internal Teams behavior.

Prefer stable Windows audio APIs.

If Teams changes its internal architecture, the application should ideally continue working because it captures the audio exposed through Windows rather than relying on Teams internals.

---

# 19. Teams Process Identification

The capture layer may need to identify the correct Teams process.

Do not hard-code a single executable/process name without validation.

Microsoft Teams has changed its desktop architecture over time.

The implementation should:

1. Detect installed/running Teams processes.
2. Identify the process responsible for the meeting audio.
3. Validate the process before capture.
4. Keep the detection logic isolated so it can be updated.

---

# 20. Multiple Teams Processes

Teams may use multiple related processes.

The application should not assume:

```text
One Teams process = One audio source
```

The proof of concept must identify which process/processes expose the meeting audio.

If multiple process audio streams are necessary, the implementation should determine the safest way to combine them.

Do not blindly capture every Teams-related process without testing.

---

# 21. Meeting Detection vs Audio Capture

These are separate responsibilities.

```text
TeamsMeetingDetector
        │
        ▼
Meeting active?
        │
        ▼
User confirmation
        │
        ▼
AudioCapture
```

Do not make audio capture responsible for deciding whether a meeting exists.

Likewise, meeting detection should not automatically start audio capture.

---

# 22. Privacy Boundary

Audio capture begins only after explicit user action.

The application should not:

- Record Teams audio continuously.
- Record microphone audio independently.
- Capture audio when the user selected **Not Now**.
- Capture audio when no note-taking session exists.

---

# 23. Recording Retention

V1 should retain the local recording unless the user chooses to delete it.

This provides a source for:

- Re-transcription.
- Verification.
- MOM correction.
- Troubleshooting.

A future setting may allow:

> **Delete recording after successful transcription**

but this is not required for the initial implementation.

---

# 24. Audio → Transcription Boundary

The audio capture module should produce a clean handoff:

```text
AudioCapture
      ↓
Completed audio file
      ↓
TranscriptionService
```

The transcription module should not need to understand how the audio was captured.

This separation makes it possible to change the capture implementation without redesigning transcription.

---

# 25. Near-Live Transcription

Near-live transcription is optional and should not compromise capture reliability.

If implemented:

```text
Audio chunk
    ↓
Transcription
    ↓
Transcript segment
```

The application must handle:

- Duplicate text.
- Chunk boundaries.
- Network delays.
- Failed chunks.
- Out-of-order responses.
- Temporary API failures.

If this complexity becomes significant, use post-meeting transcription for V1.

---

# 26. Recommended V1 Capture Strategy

The preferred development sequence is:

### Step 1

Build a small Windows/Tauri proof of concept.

### Step 2

Detect the Teams process.

### Step 3

Implement WASAPI Process Loopback Capture.

### Step 4

Capture Teams audio into WAV.

### Step 5

Test with laptop speakers.

### Step 6

Test with wired headphones.

### Step 7

Test with Bluetooth headphones.

### Step 8

Test other participant speech.

### Step 9

Test user's unmuted speech.

### Step 10

Test user's muted speech.

### Step 11

Test Teams notification/ringing sounds.

### Step 12

Test 30–120 minute recordings.

Only after these tests pass should the capture component be integrated into the full application.

---

# 27. Required Audio Test Matrix

| Scenario | Expected |
|---|---|
| Teams meeting, participant speaks | Captured |
| Teams meeting, user speaks unmuted | Captured |
| Teams meeting, user speaks muted | Not independently captured |
| Laptop speakers | Captured |
| Wired headset | Captured |
| Bluetooth headset | Captured where supported |
| No Teams meeting | No capture |
| Teams open but no meeting | No capture |

| User ends session | Capture stops |

# 28. Technical Validation Requirement

Before proceeding to full application development, the following must be proven:

> **Can Windows reliably provide the Teams meeting audio to the application without independently recording the user's physical microphone?**

This is a hard technical gate.

If the answer is yes, proceed with the planned architecture.

If the answer is partially yes, document exactly what is captured and what cannot be isolated.

If the answer is no, do not silently change the product requirement. Stop and evaluate alternative Windows audio-capture mechanisms.

---

# 29. Alternative Capture Approaches

If Process Loopback does not meet the requirement, evaluate alternatives in this order:

1. Windows Process Loopback Capture.
2. Windows WASAPI session-based capture.
3. Other supported Windows audio-session mechanisms.
4. A virtual audio device only if absolutely necessary.

Avoid virtual audio drivers for V1 unless there is no simpler reliable solution.

Do not install or depend on unnecessary third-party audio drivers.

---

# 30. No Virtual Microphone Requirement

The application does not need to provide:

- Virtual microphone.
- Virtual speaker.
- Teams audio device.
- Teams plug-in.

It should passively capture available audio.

---

# 31. No Teams Modification

The audio capture mechanism must not modify Teams.

The application should not:

- Inject code into Teams.
- Modify Teams binaries.
- Hook Teams internal functions unnecessarily.
- Alter Teams settings automatically.
- Control the user's microphone state.

The application is an external desktop utility.

---

# 32. Security Considerations

The capture module should:

- Request only necessary Windows permissions.
- Avoid capturing when not authorized by the user.
- Store recordings in the configured local location.
- Close audio resources properly.
- Avoid writing raw audio into logs.
- Never write API keys into audio metadata/logs.

---

# 33. Performance Requirements

During recording:

- Audio capture must be continuous.
- No obvious gaps should occur.
- CPU usage should remain reasonable.
- Memory usage should remain bounded.
- Disk writes should be incremental.

Audio capture should be performed in an appropriate background/native thread.

It must not block the React UI.

---

# 34. Failure Recovery

The capture component should preserve already captured audio whenever possible.

Example:

```text
00:00–35:00 captured
        ↓
Capture error
        ↓
Preserve 00:00–35:00
        ↓
Inform user
        ↓
Attempt recovery
```

Never delete the usable portion of a recording simply because the final portion failed.

---

# 35. Acceptance Criteria

The audio capture implementation is acceptable for V1 only when:

1. It can start after explicit user consent.
2. It can capture Teams meeting speech.
3. It works independently of whether the user uses speakers or headphones.
4. It captures the user's transmitted/unmuted meeting speech.
5. It does not independently capture muted/local microphone speech.
6. It does not record when no session is active.
7. It does not require a Teams bot.
8. It does not require joining the meeting.
9. It can save audio locally.
10. It can stop and finalize the recording reliably.
11. It handles common capture failures without crashing.
12. It does not consume excessive memory during long meetings.

---

# 36. Critical Development Rule

**Do not assume the audio architecture works because the code compiles.**

Audio capture must be physically tested with an actual Teams meeting.

The first proof of concept should answer one question:

> **Can the application capture exactly the useful Teams meeting audio we need?**

Only after that question is answered should the project proceed to full transcription, notes, and MOM functionality.
